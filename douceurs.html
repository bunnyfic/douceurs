sweet thingson desserts, softness & small joys

There is a moment at the end of a good meal when the table goes quiet, the plates are cleared, and something small and sweet arrives. It might be a spoonful of custard, a pastel macaron or a single strawberry under a cloud of cream. Nobody needs it, and everybody wants it. That is the whole charm of dessert.

chapter one

a brief history of sweetness

The word dessert comes from the French desservir, which means "to clear the table". It began as the course that arrived once everything else had been taken away.

For centuries sugar was a luxury, kept in locked boxes and served at feasts to impress guests. As it became easier to find, sweets became gentler and more playful: custards, pastries, little cakes dressed in ribbons and flowers. Today, dessert is less a display of wealth than a small, shared ritual of comfort.

Dessert is how a meal says, "stay a little longer."

chapter three

the small joy of sharing

The best part of dessert is rarely the sugar. It is the pause: two forks, one plate, and a few unhurried minutes. Baking for someone, or setting a pretty plate just for them, says "I thought of you" without any words at all.

Make it feel special. Use the good plate, tie a ribbon around the box, add a flower and a warm cup of something to sip.

chapter four

gentle tips for sweet makers

  • Bring eggs and butter to room temperature before you start.
  • Chill your doughs and creams. Patience is an ingredient.
  • Whip cream cold, in a cold bowl.
  • A tiny pinch of salt makes sweetness taste rounder.
  • Taste as you go, and stop mixing the moment it comes together.

Pink Vanilla Cookies

you'll need

  • ½ cup (115 g) soft butter
  • ½ cup (100 g) sugar
  • 1 egg + 1 tsp vanilla
  • 1¾ cups (220 g) flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder, pinch of salt
  • 2 tbsp strawberry powder (for the blush)

method

  1. Cream butter and sugar until fluffy.
  2. Beat in egg and vanilla.
  3. Fold in the dry ingredients; chill 30 min.
  4. Scoop onto a lined tray and bake at 175°C / 350°F for 10–12 min, until the edges are pale gold.

Dust with powdered sugar and tie a tiny ribbon on the jar.

Strawberry Cream Cake

you'll need

  • 2 eggs, ½ cup (100 g) sugar
  • 1 cup (125 g) flour, ¾ tsp baking powder
  • ¼ cup oil, ¼ cup milk, 1 tsp vanilla
  • 1 cup cold cream + 2 tbsp sugar, whipped
  • fresh strawberries

method

  1. Whisk eggs and sugar until pale and thick.
  2. Gently fold in oil, milk, then the sifted dry ingredients.
  3. Bake in a lined 8-inch pan at 175°C / 350°F for about 25 min. Cool completely.
  4. Slice in two, layer with whipped cream and berries, then crown with a swirl.
Soft Milk Bread Rolls

you'll need

  • 3 cups (375 g) bread flour
  • ⅓ cup (65 g) sugar, 1 tsp salt
  • 2¼ tsp instant yeast
  • ¾ cup warm milk, 1 egg
  • 4 tbsp soft butter

method

  1. Mix everything but the butter; knead in the butter until smooth and silky, about 10 min.
  2. Let it rise 1 hour until doubled.
  3. Shape 8 little rounds and rest 30 min.
  4. Bake at 175°C / 350°F for 18–20 min and brush with melted butter while warm.
Buldak Carbonara

you'll need

  • 1 pack buldak carbonara noodles
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 2 tbsp milk or cream
  • a handful of mozzarella or parmesan
  • crispy bacon, sliced scallion (optional)

method

  1. Boil the noodles as the packet says, then drain, keeping a few spoonfuls of the water.
  2. Return to low heat; stir in the sauce and powder.
  3. Add the milk and cheese, and stir until glossy and creamy.
  4. Top with the yolk, bacon and scallion. Mix well before the first bite.

It's spicy! Keep a cup of warm milk close by ♡
